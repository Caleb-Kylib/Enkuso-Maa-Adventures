SEASONAL

Living Alongside
the Great Migration

The Mara is a lived cultural landscape, not just a game reserve.

Every year, more than a million wildebeest and zebra sweep across the plains in one of the planet's great natural spectacles. But the Mara has never been empty wilderness. It is a landscape shaped for generations by the Maasai, whose lives continue alongside the migration.

A shared landscape

The Maasai Mara is famous worldwide for its wildlife, yet it sits within a cultural landscape whose lands, knowledge, and history are inseparable from the ecosystem. As the herds move, Maasai communities keep their own seasonal patterns of grazing and gathering — a rhythm of people, livestock, and wildlife that has coexisted here for a very long time.

Two migrations at once

While the wildebeest follow the rains in their vast loop between the Serengeti and the Mara, pastoralist families move their cattle to follow the same grass and water. Watching the plains at migration season, you are really seeing two movements overlaid — the wild and the pastoral, both reading the land in the same way.

Why local knowledge matters

Experiencing the migration alongside local guides reveals more than the river crossings the cameras chase. Generational understanding of animal behaviour and the land turns a dramatic scene into a story you can follow — where the herds are heading, why, and what it means for the season ahead.
